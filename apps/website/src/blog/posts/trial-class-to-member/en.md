A trial class is the most expensive lead you have. Someone found you, called, and showed up on the mat. Whether they join usually comes down to what happens in the next few days, not to the class itself.

Here are five habits that schools with strong trial-to-member rates tend to share.

## 1. Follow up the same day

Send a short, personal message the evening of the first class. Mention one thing the student did well and when the next class is. A message that arrives the next week feels like a sales call. One that arrives the same evening feels like a coach who noticed.

## 2. Ask what they want before you talk about price

Parents want confidence, focus or a safe way to burn energy. Adults want fitness, self-defence or a community. Ask at the front desk, write it down, and connect your recommendation to that goal. Price is easier to talk about once the family hears their own reason back.

## 3. Book the second class before they leave

The second visit matters more than the first. Before the student walks out, agree on a day and time for the next class and put it in your schedule. A booked class is a plan. "See you sometime this week" is a maybe.

## 4. Make the first goal visible

New students do not know what progress looks like yet. Show them the first stripe or the first belt and what it takes to get there. A clear, near goal turns "trying it out" into "working towards something".

## 5. Bring the family in

For kids, the parent is the real decision maker. Invite them to watch the last ten minutes, introduce them to the instructor by name, and tell them what their child learned. Parents who feel part of the school stay longer.

## Keep it simple

None of this needs a big system. It needs a list of trial students, a reminder to follow up, and someone who owns it each day. Write down who came in, when you followed up and when they are booked next, and review the list at the end of every week.

> The best trial follow-up is the one that actually happens, every time.
