Belt testing is the day students work towards and families remember. It is also the day a small school is most likely to feel stretched. A little preparation turns it from a scramble into a celebration.

## Three to four weeks before

- Decide who is ready. Check attendance, time at their current rank and the instructor's notes, not just who asked.
- Tell families early: the date, the time, what to wear and what the testing fee covers.
- Order belts, stripes and certificates for everyone who is testing, with a few spares.
- Pick who does what on the day: who runs the floor, who judges, who welcomes families.

## The week before

- Send a reminder with the schedule and arrival time.
- Run a practice round in regular classes so nothing on the day is a surprise.
- Print the judging sheets and the order students will test in.
- Check that every testing fee is settled, so nobody has that conversation on the day.

## On the day

1. Open the doors early and seat families before the first group starts.
2. Start on time and keep each group short. Long waits are hard on young students.
3. Explain what the judges are looking for, so parents know what they are watching.
4. Finish with the belt ceremony and give every student a moment in front of their family.

## After the test

- Update each student's rank and the date in your records the same day.
- Share photos only of students whose families agreed.
- Send a short note of congratulations to every family.
- For anyone who needs more time, give them a clear plan for the next test, in private.

## Why it matters

A well-run test tells every family that the school takes their child's progress seriously. That feeling is a big part of why students stay for the next belt, and the one after that.
